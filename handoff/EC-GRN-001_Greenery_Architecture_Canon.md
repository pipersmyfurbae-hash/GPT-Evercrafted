## Purpose
Greenery is the architecture of the wreath. It creates movement, depth, transition, framing, and pockets that later receive floral elements.

## Core Laws
1. Greenery architecture is established before floral placement.
2. Greenery defines movement; florals reinforce it.
3. Greenery creates pockets; flowers inhabit those pockets.
4. Greenery establishes depth through forward, middle, and receding relationships.
5. Greenery connects the composition and prevents isolated clusters.
6. Do not blanket the wreath in greenery and then cover that greenery with flowers.
7. Use only the material needed to make the structural gesture readable.
8. Material efficiency and visual refinement support each other.
9. Behavior is selected before species.
10. Species/material selection must satisfy the required behavior, season, palette, inventory, and construction needs.

## Room / Architecture Analogy
Shape the room first; furnish it second.

The greenery establishes the room:
- boundaries;
- circulation;
- openings;
- support;
- depth;
- pockets.

Flowers furnish the room. They do not replace the architecture.

## Behavior-First Selection Mechanism
1. Read emotional/design intent.
2. Determine required structural behavior.
3. Describe visual characteristics without naming a species.
4. Match available materials/species capable of producing that behavior.
5. Validate season, palette, inventory, scale, and construction practicality.

This prevents rules such as “nostalgia = eucalyptus.” Instead, nostalgia might require a gentle drape, restrained motion, soft transitions, or another behavior. Species is chosen afterward.

## Greenery Behavior Library

### GRN-B01 — Cascading
**Purpose:** Controlled downward movement.

**Geometry:** Curving or arcing stems that gradually descend rather than dropping as a stiff vertical line.

**Visual Effect:** Softens and lengthens the composition, guiding the eye away from an anchor.

**Construction Rule:** Use the minimum amount of material needed to make the downward gesture readable.

**Pocket Relationship:** May create the boundary of floral pockets so blooms emerge from within the architecture.

### GRN-B02 — Sweeping
**Purpose:** Carry the eye laterally around the wreath form and connect zones.

**Geometry:** Long directional movement following the circular form.

**Visual Effect:** Graceful momentum without becoming a dense band.

**Construction Rule:** Thin, readable gesture; never a hedge of greenery.

**Pocket Relationship:** Connects pockets along its path without filling them.

### GRN-B03 — Arching
**Purpose:** Create upward or outward lift and frame open space.

**Geometry:** Rising curved lines.

**Visual Effect:** Openness, invitation, lift, and breathing room.

**Construction Rule:** The arch supports or frames space without becoming vertical dominance.

### GRN-B04 — Bridging
**Purpose:** Connect two pockets or movements subtly.

**Geometry:** Small connective gestures across a transition.

**Visual Effect:** Continuity without erasing negative space.

**Construction Rule:** Stitching, not scaffolding. If the bridge becomes a focal feature, it is too heavy.

### GRN-B05 — Nesting
**Purpose:** Cradle a flower or micro-cluster so it feels held and secure.

**Geometry:** Curving structure around a floral pocket.

**Visual Effect:** Protection, softness, rest, and resolution.

**Construction Rule:** Support the pocket without creating a rigid ring around it.

**Resolution Note:** Nesting is a major cue that the florals belong to the architecture.

### GRN-B06 — Framing
**Purpose:** Define the edge or boundary of the visual story.

**Geometry:** Explicit or implied boundary lines.

**Visual Effect:** Clarifies where composition begins and ends while supporting the emotional anchor.

**Construction Rule:** Readable edge with minimal material; never compete with the anchor.

## Cost / Material Principle
Architecture-first construction should avoid unnecessary material hidden beneath other materials. This supports:
- lower material waste;
- better cost control;
- more visible handcraft;
- better depth;
- a florist-made, interwoven appearance rather than a layered-on appearance.
