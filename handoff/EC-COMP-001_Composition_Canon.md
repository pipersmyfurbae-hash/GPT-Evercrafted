## Purpose
Composition is the visual structure through which the memory is experienced. It is defined before specific flowers are chosen.

## Foundational Laws
1. Every design establishes a clear emotional anchor.
2. The anchor is the beginning of the eye path, not the entire story.
3. Every composition defines an intentional visual journey.
4. Every journey contains moments of rest.
5. Negative space is an active design element.
6. Greenery creates architecture; flowers inhabit that architecture.
7. Flowers reinforce movement that already exists instead of inventing it independently.
8. Every flower must belong to the architecture through overlap, depth, transition, or relationship.
9. Every element must earn its place.
10. Elements must be in visual conversation with other elements.
11. Composition is relationships, not coordinates.
12. A design is not complete merely because every area is filled.

## Composition Gravity
Every design has a center of visual/emotional gravity. This is not necessarily the geometric center.

Emotion may influence whether composition gravity feels:
- grounded;
- lifted;
- outward;
- inward;
- stable;
- expanding;
- quiet;
- energized.

Composition gravity is established before species selection.

## Construction Sequence
1. Establish emotional anchor.
2. Define visual path.
3. Define rest points and negative space.
4. Establish composition gravity.
5. Build greenery architecture.
6. Create pockets.
7. Assign floral roles inside the architecture.
8. Integrate overlap and depth.
9. Add balancing/echo relationships.
10. Refine and remove anything that weakens the story.

## Validation Questions
A composition is not ready unless:
- Is the emotional anchor clear?
- Does the eye know where to enter?
- Is there an intentional path after entry?
- Are there meaningful rest points?
- Is negative space deliberate?
- Does the greenery establish structure?
- Do the flowers feel held by the structure?
- Is visual weight balanced without requiring mirror symmetry?
- Does every major element have a reason?
- Are isolated elements corrected or removed?

## Resolution Principle
A composition feels resolved when the florals feel held, supported, and secure within the greenery architecture rather than sitting on top of the wreath.
